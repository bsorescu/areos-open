# B2B & Regulated-Domain Nuances

For buyers under liability, regulation, or audit pressure: building/facility
administrators, legal & compliance officers, fintech, safety, medical. They
answer to authorities and their careers ride on vendor choices — **trust is
the conversion currency; anything that spends trust to gain clicks is
net-negative.**

## How the standard biases change

| Bias | B2C default | Regulated-B2B application |
|---|---|---|
| Framing (loss > gain) | Amplify the pain | State the *documented* obligation and its real consequence, with the legal source. No dramatization: "Art. X requires Y; missing it risks Z fine" — not "you could end up in prison". Fear beyond the sourced fact reads as manipulation and kills credibility. |
| Urgency / fresh start | Countdown, seasonal hooks | Only real regulatory dates (enforcement deadlines, inspection cycles). These are stronger than any manufactured urgency — use them, sourced. |
| Scarcity | "Only N left" | Almost never applicable. Capacity limits only if operationally true and verifiable. |
| Discounts | Splashy promos | Restraint. Heavy discounting signals desperation and cheapens a risk-mitigation product. Prefer value justification (Fairness: "because...") over price cuts; frame annual plans as "2 months free", not slashed prices. |
| Humor (Wisdom of Wit) | Empty states, emails, 404s | Default OFF in copy about risk, liability, fines, inspections, incidents. Acceptable in low-stakes UI corners (404, changelog) if the brand has earned warmth. Never joke about the failure mode your product prevents. |
| Social proof | Volume ("10k users") | Specificity beats volume: named peers, case numbers, auditor/authority acceptance. 12 real named-type pilots > "hundreds of users". |
| Red Sneakers | Edgy positioning | Only with established credibility; regulators and risk-averse buyers read convention-breaking as risk. |

## Tone

Sober, precise, verifiable. Write like the document could be read aloud in
an audit — because in this segment, it might be.

## Proof hierarchy (strongest first)

1. Authority/auditor acceptance ("export accepted in N ISU inspections")
2. Named peer usage or case study with numbers
3. Precise operational facts ("tracks 47 document types")
4. Team credentials / effort shown (Illusion of Effort)
Generic claims ("trusted", "leading") carry zero weight here — cut them.
