# Segment & Awareness Adaptation

Two axes set at diagnostic time (stage 1); copy that ignores either
converts the wrong people or nobody.

## Awareness levels (Schwartz) — what the copy must do first

| Level | Buyer state | Copy's first job | Typical artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know the problem exists | Name the problem concretely (Generation Effect: questions) | Content, lead magnet |
| Problem-aware | Feels the pain, no solution known | Agitate *verified* pain → introduce solution category | Landing hero, ads |
| Solution-aware | Knows the category, not you | Differentiate: positioning claim + proof | Landing body, comparison pages |
| Product-aware | Knows you, unconvinced | Objection handling (objection-matrix.md) + proof hierarchy | Pricing page, case studies |
| Most-aware | Ready, needs a reason now | Real deadline / real offer + friction removal | Checkout, trial-expiry email |

Mismatch symptom: hero copy selling features (product-aware register) on a
cold-traffic page (problem-aware audience) — or urgency tactics on unaware
visitors who don't yet care.

## Segment adaptation

- Same product, different buyer = different research doc rows: a building
  administrator (liability-driven) and a property owner (cost-driven) need
  different primary messages even for one landing page — split pages or
  split sections, don't average them.
- Awareness level is per-segment, per-channel: retargeting traffic is more
  aware than cold social traffic; email list ≠ visitors.
- Record segment × awareness in the diagnostic; every playbook consumes it.
